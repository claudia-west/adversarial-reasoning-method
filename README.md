# The Adversarial Reasoning Method

**A conceptual framework proposal emphasizing structured diagnostic reasoning, deliberate reflection, and long-term pattern integration.**

## Overview

The Adversarial Reasoning Method (ARM) is a proposed eight-step framework for structured clinical diagnostic reasoning and diagnostic-error research:

**Observe > Represent > Compete > Attack > Challenge > Update > Reflect > Integrate**

ARM organizes previously separate, previously published reasoning traditions (Bordage, Kassirer, Croskerry, Mamede & Schmidt) into one sequenced procedure, converts a situational debiasing tool into a mandatory step, and closes the loop with explicit post-case Reflect and Integrate stages.

**ARM is a conceptual proposal and has not yet been empirically validated.** The manuscript does not claim that ARM reduces diagnostic error or improves diagnostic accuracy. Its claimed contribution is narrower: sequencing, mandatory placement of the adversarial step, and the Reflect/Integrate stages, not the individual reasoning operations themselves, which are drawn from existing literature and cited as such.

## The framework

1. Observe | Structured intake of clinical data, separated from interpretation | Addresses incomplete data gathering
2. Represent | Compression of findings into a concise, pattern-oriented summary | Addresses diagnostic ambiguity
3. Compete | Generation of competing hypotheses with supporting/contradicting evidence | Addresses premature closure
4. Attack | Adversarial audit of the leading hypothesis via structured disconfirming questions | Addresses confirmation bias, anchoring
5. Challenge | Test selection based on expected impact on the leading decision | Addresses low-value testing
6. Update | Iterative revision as new data accumulate | Addresses diagnostic inertia
7. Reflect | Structured, socially embedded post-case self-evaluation | Addresses failure to learn
8. Integrate | Long-term pattern formation across cases | Addresses passive experience accumulation

## Current status

**Status: Conceptual framework proposal; not empirically validated.**

The manuscript proposes a small-scale case-series feasibility pilot as a precursor to simulation-based and randomized evaluation. ARM should be treated as a research proposal, not an established clinical method.

## Research program

ARM is currently at the conceptual-specification stage. Proposed next steps:

1. Case-series feasibility pilot (5–8 vignettes, 2–4 raters)
2. Volume-vs-depth and rigor/throughput evaluation
3. Simulation-based cohort study
4. Randomized controlled evaluation

Each stage is gated on the success of the one before it.

## Limitations

ARM is conceptual and has not been empirically validated. Several of its eight steps are, by the manuscript's own account, procedural restatements of previously published work rather than novel cognitive mechanisms. The manuscript is explicit about which parts are new (sequencing, mandatory Attack, Reflect/Integrate) and which are not. Open questions include whether the sequence improves reasoning outcomes, whether documentation burden trades off against case volume, and whether Reflect/Integrate produces measurable longitudinal benefit. These are treated as questions for empirical testing, not resolved by argument.
